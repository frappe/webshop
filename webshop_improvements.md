# ERPNext Webshop vs. OPD’s E-commerce Platform – Capabilities & Gap Analysis

## Overview of OPD’s Current E-commerce Features

Office Products Depot’s website (opd.co.nz) is a feature-rich B2B/B2C e-commerce site with a broad catalog and multiple branch locations. Key characteristics of the current site include:

- **Extensive Product Hierarchy:** A deep multi-level category menu is used for navigation. For example, “Office Supplies” is a top-level category, with sub-categories like “Adhesives, Glues and Tapes,” which further break down into specific product groups (Double Sided Tapes, Super Glues, etc.) [oai_citation:0‡opd.co.nz](https://opd.co.nz/?srsltid=AfmBOop8_Sh2OLOFQyFUMSVoLSWn1QM7L54FMu8Nq1Fvw_6QtHfji4_8#:~:text=,4) [oai_citation:1‡opd.co.nz](https://opd.co.nz/?srsltid=AfmBOop8_Sh2OLOFQyFUMSVoLSWn1QM7L54FMu8Nq1Fvw_6QtHfji4_8#:~:text=Filing%2C%20Storage%20and%20Dividers). This hierarchical menu allows users to drill down through categories and sub-categories to find products.
- **Multiple Branches / Store Selection:** The site caters to numerous independently operated branches. A **“Choose Store”** option is provided, and the footer lists all the local branch stores with addresses and contacts [oai_citation:2‡opd.co.nz](https://opd.co.nz/product/fellowes-90m-automax-shredder-mini-cut/7110845#:~:text=Our%20Stores) [oai_citation:3‡opd.co.nz](https://opd.co.nz/product/fellowes-90m-automax-shredder-mini-cut/7110845#:~:text=Get%20Directions). Pricing is presented as **recommended retail price (RRP)**, with a note that *actual prices may vary by local dealer* [oai_citation:4‡opd.co.nz](https://opd.co.nz/product/fellowes-90m-automax-shredder-mini-cut/7110845#:~:text=EACH). This suggests that after selecting a branch (or logging into a specific branch account), customers might see branch-specific pricing or promotions.
- **Customer Accounts & B2B Features:** Users can sign in to their account (e.g. corporate customers likely have accounts). The **“My Account”** section allows users to view their details and presumably past orders. Payment terms for account customers are mentioned in terms (e.g. payment by 20th of next month for invoiced orders) [oai_citation:5‡opd.co.nz](https://opd.co.nz/terms-conditions?srsltid=AfmBOoqXxK9y7gZ-2osABD37hNIHHTDDzmVqRsRKFKqcI7uPCNC8nNlx#:~:text=Terms%20%26%20Conditions%20,with%20effect%20from%20the). The site likely supports placing orders on account for approved customers, in addition to online payment for general users.
- **Online Payment Options:** The OPD site accepts credit card payments via the **Windcave** payment gateway [oai_citation:6‡direct.opd.co.nz](https://direct.opd.co.nz/#:~:text=) (Windcave is a popular NZ payment provider). The footer “We Accept” section displays the Windcave logo alongside credit card icons, indicating online card payment is supported. This is in addition to offline invoicing for business account holders.
- **Shopping Cart & Checkout:** Customers can add items to a cart and either proceed to checkout or (if not logged in) possibly submit an order inquiry. (If a user is not logged in, the site may restrict checkout – this is common for B2B setups. ERPNext can be configured similarly to require login or allow guest checkouts, discussed later.)
- **Product Pages & Info:** Each product page shows detailed information: multiple images, product code, brand, stock availability, price (noting “Ex GST” since NZ prices often exclude tax), product specifications, and descriptive details [oai_citation:7‡opd.co.nz](https://opd.co.nz/product/fellowes-90m-automax-shredder-mini-cut/7110845#:~:text=FELLOWES%2090M%20AUTOMAX%20SHREDDER%20MINI,CUT) [oai_citation:8‡opd.co.nz](https://opd.co.nz/product/fellowes-90m-automax-shredder-mini-cut/7110845#:~:text=Details). OPD’s pages also highlight that prices exclude GST and that they are RRP reference prices [oai_citation:9‡opd.co.nz](https://opd.co.nz/product/fellowes-90m-automax-shredder-mini-cut/7110845#:~:text=EACH). There are no customer reviews on OPD, as it’s primarily a supply store model (reviews/ratings are not present on the site).
- **Search and Filtering:** The site provides powerful filtering and search capabilities. There is an “All Products” or category listing view that shows a sidebar of filters by category, brand, color, and various product attributes. For example, on an “All Products” listing, one can filter by *Item Group* (category), *Brand*, *Color*, *Size*, etc., with item counts in parentheses [oai_citation:10‡opd.co.nz](https://opd.co.nz/inkandtoner#:~:text=) [oai_citation:11‡opd.co.nz](https://opd.co.nz/inkandtoner#:~:text=Filter%20by%20Brand). Users can search for products (the UI has a search bar for keywords or product codes). OPD even has a dedicated “Ink and Toner” finder section, allowing users to find printer cartridges by printer or cartridge type, which is essentially a filtered view of products in the “Inks and Toners” category [oai_citation:12‡opd.co.nz](https://opd.co.nz/inkandtoner#:~:text=,22) [oai_citation:13‡opd.co.nz](https://opd.co.nz/inkandtoner#:~:text=match%20at%20L547%20Image%3A%20BROTHER,TN3415%20TONER%20CARTRIDGE%20BLACK).
- **Marketing Content:** The homepage includes marketing banners (slideshow), “Trending Now” featured products, “Popular categories” links, and a “Shop by Brand” section with brand logos. These are likely manually curated sections highlighting certain items and categories. There are also sections for *Latest Catalogues* (possibly linking to digital catalogs) and *Special Offers/Clearance* items [oai_citation:14‡direct.opd.co.nz](https://direct.opd.co.nz/#:~:text=). These features indicate the site has customizable content sections beyond just a product grid (likely managed by their platform or custom development).
- **Theme and Branding:** The OPD site has a custom theme reflecting their brand colors and style. It’s a clean, corporate look with a responsive design. Any new solution should allow significant theme customization to present a similarly polished, branded interface.

In summary, OPD’s current platform provides a **full e-commerce experience**: multi-level category navigation, advanced product filtering, account-based pricing considerations, multiple payment options (including local gateway), and the ability to showcase content (banners, catalogs, offers) in a flexible way. The goal is to replicate or improve upon these capabilities using **ERPNext’s Webshop** (ERPNext’s built-in e-commerce module, now available as the separate Frappe Webshop app) integrated with ERPNext’s ERP features.

## ERPNext & Frappe Webshop – Out-of-the-Box E-commerce Capabilities

ERPNext, coupled with the Frappe framework, includes a robust **Website and E-commerce module**. Recently, the e-commerce module has been refactored into a separate application called **Frappe Webshop**, which can be installed alongside ERPNext. This provides the online storefront, while ERPNext handles backend ERP functions (products, inventory, orders, customers, etc.). Out-of-the-box, this combination offers many features that align closely with OPD’s needs:

- **Online Product Catalog:** ERPNext allows publishing items to a web storefront (via a *Website Item* doctype or similar). You can present an **“All Products” page (`/all-products`)** that lists all items or filtered subsets [oai_citation:15‡docs.frappe.io](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/item_group_wise_product_listing#:~:text=Item%20Group). Products can be organized by categories (using **Item Groups** as categories). ERPNext supports hierarchical item groups, and you can enable *Item Group-wise product listing* pages for each top-level category [oai_citation:16‡docs.frappe.io](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/item_group_wise_product_listing#:~:text=Similar%20to%20the%20Product%20Listing,page%20to%20direct%20users%20easily). When a category has sub-groups, the website will show those as clickable filters or “pills” for navigation [oai_citation:17‡docs.frappe.io](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/item_group_wise_product_listing#:~:text=,their%20Website%20Item%20Groups%20table). In essence, multi-level categories are supported – you can create a hierarchy of Item Groups (e.g., Office Supplies ▸ Adhesives ▸ Glue Tapes), and customers can navigate accordingly.

- **Advanced Search & Filtering:** The webshop has built-in support for faceted filtering and search on product listings. In **E-Commerce Settings**, administrators can designate certain Item fields as **Field Filters** (for example, Brand, Color, or any custom attribute) and enable **Attribute Filters** for item variant attributes [oai_citation:18‡docs.frappe.io](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/e_commerce_settings#:~:text=You%20can%20also%20add%20filters,are%20two%20types%20of%20filters). These configured filters will appear on the storefront allowing users to refine by those criteria. For instance, you can enable filters by Brand, by Color, by Size, etc., exactly as seen on the OPD site. The system will automatically list the distinct values and item counts for each filter (as illustrated on OPD). An on-page search bar is also available for keyword search within products. Overall, the **search and filtering experience is quite powerful**, matching modern e-commerce standards [oai_citation:19‡github.com](https://github.com/frappe/webshop#:~:text=,ERPNext%20allows%20for%20management%20of) [oai_citation:20‡github.com](https://github.com/frappe/webshop#:~:text=of%20their%20orders.%20,of%20use%2C%20with%20a%20focus).

- **Product Detail Pages:** Each product (Website Item) page displays the item name, code/SKU, images (multiple images supported), description, specifications, stock availability, and pricing. **Stock availability** and even exact stock quantity can be shown if desired (configurable) [oai_citation:21‡docs.frappe.io](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/e_commerce_settings#:~:text=1,to%20be%20added%20to%20Cart). Pricing shown can be drawn from a designated Price List (e.g., standard selling rate) and can dynamically reflect discounts or variant price differences. The interface supports **product variants** – if an item has multiple variants (size, color, etc.), the product page can show a “Configure” or variant selection option to pick the specific variant [oai_citation:22‡docs.frappe.io](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/e_commerce_settings#:~:text=Display%20Settings). This ensures that even complex products with options are handled seamlessly.

- **Shopping Cart & Checkout Workflow:** Frappe Webshop provides a full shopping cart. Customers can add items to a cart and then proceed to checkout to place an order. In ERPNext’s settings, you can choose to enable full **Checkout** or use a **Quotation (RFQ) mode**. If *Checkout is enabled*, placing an order generates a Sales Order directly and requires payment (via integration with a payment gateway) [oai_citation:23‡docs.frappe.io](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/e_commerce_settings#:~:text=1,you%20can%20choose%20whether%20or). If checkout is disabled (e.g., for B2B scenarios), the cart prompts a “Request for Quotation” instead, which creates a Quotation in ERPNext for follow-up [oai_citation:24‡docs.frappe.io](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/e_commerce_settings#:~:text=Checkout%20Settings). This flexibility is useful – for B2B clients like OPD’s, you might initially allow RFQ or invoice-based orders for account customers, whereas for retail you enable immediate online payment.

- **Payment Gateway Integration:** ERPNext supports integration with popular payment gateways out of the box. Specifically, PayPal and Razorpay integrations are built-in and easily configured [oai_citation:25‡docs.frappe.io](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/e_commerce_settings#:~:text=it,disabled%2C%20on%20clicking%20on%20the). Frappe Webshop extends this with support for **Stripe** as well [oai_citation:26‡github.com](https://github.com/frappe/webshop#:~:text=can%20be%20easily%20tracked.%20,customers%20save%20their%20favorites%20and), and it’s designed to allow adding other gateways as needed. (We’ll discuss Windcave integration in the gaps section.) Multiple payment options can be offered – for example, PayPal for international, Stripe for credit card – and these provide secure, convenient transactions [oai_citation:27‡github.com](https://github.com/frappe/webshop#:~:text=can%20be%20easily%20tracked.%20,the%20status%20of%20their%20orders). If no online payment is configured, you can still allow order placement (Sales Order) and then handle payment offline (e.g., invoice the customer).

- **Customer Accounts and Portal:** ERPNext has a built-in **web portal** for customers. When a user signs up or is invited as a website user (and linked to a Customer record), they can log in to view their profile and past transactions (orders, invoices, etc.). The portal can show **Order History, Invoice history, and even Delivery status** to the customer, improving transparency [oai_citation:28‡kiktronik.com](https://www.kiktronik.com/understanding-erpnext-customer-portals-a-practical-guide-for-medium-sized-businesses#:~:text=,transparency%20and%20reducing%20administrative) [oai_citation:29‡multisaber.angolaerp.co.ao](https://multisaber.angolaerp.co.ao/docs/user/manual/en/customer-portal/customer-orders-invoices-and-shipping-status#:~:text=ERPNext%20Web%20Portal%20gives%20your,their%20orders%2C%20invoices%2C%20and). Frappe Webshop leverages this – **User Accounts** allow customers to save information for faster checkouts, maintain wishlists, and track their orders in the portal [oai_citation:30‡github.com](https://github.com/frappe/webshop#:~:text=can%20be%20easily%20tracked.%20,the%20status%20of%20their%20orders) [oai_citation:31‡github.com](https://github.com/frappe/webshop#:~:text=allowing%20for%20convenient%20checkout%20and,can%20share%20their%20experiences%20and). This fulfills the need for a “My Account” section similar to OPD’s, and being tied to the ERP means real-time order status and data are available to the user.

- **Shopping Enhancements:** The platform includes modern e-commerce conveniences:
  - **Wishlist/Favorites:** Customers (when logged in) can add items to a wishlist to save for later [oai_citation:32‡github.com](https://github.com/frappe/webshop#:~:text=Paypal%20or%20Stripe%2C%20allowing%20secure,the%20status%20of%20their%20orders). This is optional (configurable via E-Commerce Settings) [oai_citation:33‡docs.frappe.io](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/e_commerce_settings#:~:text=Add).
  - **Product Reviews & Ratings:** You can allow logged-in customers to leave reviews/ratings on products [oai_citation:34‡docs.frappe.io](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/e_commerce_settings#:~:text=1,items%20against%20a%20Website%20Item). This is a built-in feature that can be enabled if desired (though OPD doesn’t use it, it’s available if needed).
  - **Discount/Coupon Codes:** The checkout has an option to apply a coupon code for discounts [oai_citation:35‡docs.frappe.io](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/e_commerce_settings#:~:text=6,which%20customers%20can%20use%20to). This works in conjunction with ERPNext’s pricing rules or a coupon system (you’d configure a Discount Coupon code in the backend and the system will apply the corresponding pricing rule or discount if the code is valid).
  - **Related Products:** ERPNext can show manually curated “Recommended items” on a product page if configured [oai_citation:36‡docs.frappe.io](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/e_commerce_settings#:~:text=2,items%20against%20a%20Website%20Item). You can link other items as recommendations (e.g., accessories or alternatives), which then display to the customer.
  - **“Shop by Category” Page:** There is an out-of-the-box **Shop By Category** page that can list all top-level categories with a banner, helping users navigate broadly [oai_citation:37‡docs.frappe.io](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/e_commerce_settings#:~:text=Shop%20By%20Category). This page’s contents are driven by the categories (Item Groups) you mark for website display.

- **Content Management & Pages:** Even before introducing the new Builder, ERPNext’s website module allows creation of static pages (Web Pages), blogs, and custom banners:
  - You can create **custom web pages** (e.g., an “About Us” or “Contact Us” page) through the desk without coding – using the text editor or by embedding custom HTML if needed.
  - For dynamic content like a rotating banner on the homepage, ERPNext has a **Website Slideshow** feature where you can define slides (images and text) and set them in Website Settings to appear on the homepage.
  - **Web Forms** can be created for things like a contact form, newsletter signup, etc., which is useful for capturing leads or newsletter subscriptions (OPD’s “Signup for local promotions” could be implemented as a Web Form for newsletter subscription).

- **Theming and UI Customization:** Frappe/ERPNext provides a **Website Theme** feature that makes it possible to customize the look and feel of the site without hard-coding styles. Admins can create a new theme in the UI and adjust the basic color scheme, fonts, and button styles through a simple form [oai_citation:38‡docs.frappe.io](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/website-theme#:~:text=The%20section%20,scheme%2C%20font%20and%20button%20styles). For deeper customization, the theme allows insertion of custom SCSS (Sass CSS) to override Bootstrap variables or add new CSS rules [oai_citation:39‡docs.frappe.io](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/website-theme#:~:text=If%20you%20know%20SCSS%20and,can%20manually%20write%20custom%20SCSS). This means you can implement corporate branding (colors, spacing, etc.) by overriding the default theme’s variables – for example, set your primary color, tweak spacing, or change font family globally [oai_citation:40‡docs.frappe.io](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/website-theme#:~:text=theming%20getbootstrap,manually%20write%20custom%20SCSS). The theme system compiles these changes and applies them site-wide. You can even include/exclude the built-in theme files of each app (ERPNext, Webshop, etc.) if you want to drop in an entirely custom stylesheet [oai_citation:41‡docs.frappe.io](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/website-theme#:~:text=If%20you%20take%20a%20look,file). In short, **ERPNext’s theming capability is quite powerful and designed to allow substantial re-skinning of the site** without editing core code.

- **Modern User Interface:** The default storefront provided by Frappe Webshop is clean and responsive. It includes both **List view and Grid view** toggle for products, a sidebar filter panel, and AJAX-based cart updates. For example, the screenshot below shows an *All Products* page with filter facets (by Item Group, Brand, Size, Color, etc.), a search bar, and product listings with “Add to Cart” buttons and wishlist hearts: 

   *Example of ERPNext Webshop product listing with a filter sidebar and product cards. The interface includes search, category filtering, and quick add-to-cart buttons [oai_citation:42‡github.com](https://github.com/frappe/webshop#:~:text=,ERPNext%20allows%20for%20management%20of) [oai_citation:43‡docs.frappe.io](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/e_commerce_settings#:~:text=1,you%20want%20to%20have%20filters).*

  This default UI can be a starting point and can be further tailored through theming or overriding templates if necessary.

- **Integration with ERP (All-in-One Solution):** A major advantage of using ERPNext+Webshop is that the **e-commerce front-end is fully integrated with the backend ERP**. Product information (description, stock, price) comes straight from the ERPNext item master and stock ledgers – ensuring that the website always shows *real-time stock availability and pricing*. When an online order is placed, it creates a Sales Order (and Payment Entry if paid) directly in ERPNext. There is no need for a separate e-commerce platform and manual syncing of products or orders; everything is in one system. This addresses the prospect’s point about an all-in-one ERP+eCommerce solution and will likely simplify operations and reduce errors (no data silo). Pricing strategies (customer-specific pricing, discount schemes) set in the ERP are reflected on the site. **This integration is a strong selling point** versus having a separate website platform.

In summary, **ERPNext with Frappe Webshop provides a comprehensive out-of-the-box e-commerce feature set**: multi-level categories and filtering, flexible cart/checkout (with or without immediate payment), account management, and integration into ERP processes (inventory and sales). Many of the capabilities observed on OPD’s site – like category navigation, product filters, cart, and basic theme customization – are supported natively. Next, we’ll compare specific features and identify gaps where Frappe Webshop may need enhancements or configuration to match OPD’s current functionality.

## Feature Comparison and Gaps Analysis

While ERPNext’s e-commerce framework is feature-rich, there are a few areas where we need to validate fit or consider custom enhancements to meet all of OPD’s current features. Below is a comparison of key aspects and identification of gaps:

### 1. **Category Navigation & Hierarchy**

**OPD Current Site:** Supports deep category hierarchies with a mega-menu style navigation (multiple levels of drop-down). Users can browse by main departments (Office Supplies, Furniture, Technology, etc.), then sub-categories, etc. The UI on OPD is a left sidebar menu on desktop and likely a mega menu or accordion on mobile. It’s very user-friendly for exploring a large catalog.

**ERPNext Webshop:** **Supports multi-level categories** through Item Groups. Each Item Group can be shown on the website and has its own listing page [oai_citation:44‡docs.frappe.io](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/item_group_wise_product_listing#:~:text=To%20provide%20a%20Product%20Listing,Products%29%2C%20go%20to). Child groups are displayed on the parent page as clickable links or “pills” for navigation [oai_citation:45‡docs.frappe.io](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/item_group_wise_product_listing#:~:text=,their%20Website%20Item%20Groups%20table). However, the default website navigation menu in ERPNext is not automatically a mega-menu; you might need to manually set up top navigation links. Typically, one could put a link to the “All Products” page or main category pages in the top menu. The actual category structure tends to be exposed via the **Filters sidebar** (as in the screenshot above) rather than a hover dropdown menu. 

**Gap:** If a mega-menu style top navigation is required (with hover drop-down of subcategories), some front-end customization would be needed. Out-of-the-box, the user might need to click “All Products” and then use filters, or you create static top menu entries for each major category. This is more of a **UX enhancement** than missing functionality. To implement a similar navigation to OPD:
- **Option A:** Utilize the existing **“Shop by Category”** page as the main gateway – it will list all categories in a nicely formatted way by default [oai_citation:46‡docs.frappe.io](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/e_commerce_settings#:~:text=Shop%20By%20Category). We can link this in the main nav as “Products” (like OPD does).
- **Option B:** Build a custom navigation bar using the **Website Menu** doctype or custom HTML in the theme. We could manually create a drop-down structure reflecting the Item Group tree.
- **Option C:** Leverage **Frappe Builder** to design a custom mega-menu component that can be reused. Builder’s component system could allow creating a dynamic menu that pulls category data (since it can generate dynamic blocks from data) [oai_citation:47‡frappe.io](https://frappe.io/builder#:~:text=sections%20that%20represent%20dynamic%20objects%2C,the%20backend%20and%20show%20them), though this might be advanced usage.

**Priority:** Medium. It’s important for demo/usability, but even if the initial demo uses a simpler category navigation (sidebar filters on an All Products page), it would still demonstrate the capability. For a polished product, we’d likely implement a friendlier top menu or landing page with category cards (which can be done easily – see Builder usage below).

### 2. **Product Filters & Search**

**OPD:** Has an extensive set of filters (Brand, Color, etc.) visible on category pages, and a search bar. The filters allow narrow-down by multiple attributes and clearly show count of results.

**ERPNext:** As noted, it natively supports field and attribute filters on product listing pages [oai_citation:48‡docs.frappe.io](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/e_commerce_settings#:~:text=You%20can%20also%20add%20filters,are%20two%20types%20of%20filters). You can replicate all needed filters by configuring them in **E-Commerce Settings**. For example, add “Brand” as a Field Filter to get a *Filter by Brand* section, add “Color” (if you have such a field or as a variant attribute) to get a *Filter by Color*, etc. The screenshot embedded earlier actually demonstrates filters by Brand, Size, Color, etc., which is exactly what OPD uses. Search is also built-in (the “Search for products” bar).

**Gap:** Very little gap here – the functionality is largely equivalent. One thing to verify is whether ERPNext’s filters allow multi-select (choosing multiple values in one filter category). As of now, the filters UI lets you click one value at a time (and possibly combine with another filter type). If OPD’s allows multi-select (e.g., choose two brands at once), that might not be supported out-of-box. This is a minor UX difference; we can live with single-select filters if needed. If multi-select is crucial, a custom tweak in the JS could be made.

**Priority:** Low gap – core capability is there. Just need to configure the filters properly. In preparation for the demo, we should populate item attributes/fields (Brand, etc.) in ERPNext and set them as filters so the demo site replicates the OPD filtering experience.

### 3. **Multiple Branches / Store Selection**

**OPD:** Highly emphasizes choosing a local branch (“Choose Store”) and mentions that pricing and availability might differ by branch [oai_citation:49‡opd.co.nz](https://opd.co.nz/product/fellowes-90m-automax-shredder-mini-cut/7110845#:~:text=EACH). It lists numerous branch contacts, implying a federated structure of independent dealers under one brand. Likely, when a customer logs in or selects a store, their orders are fulfilled by that store and possibly at that store’s pricing. This is a *complex multi-company scenario*.

**ERPNext:** Out-of-the-box, ERPNext can handle **multiple companies or branches** in one instance (multi-company accounting and multi-warehouse inventory). However, the **website module by default does not have a built-in store-selector for end-users**. The site is generally one store (one company) at a time in terms of pricing. There is a single price list configured for the web shop prices [oai_citation:50‡docs.frappe.io](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/e_commerce_settings#:~:text=1,customers%20on%20adding%20to%20Cart), and a single “company” context for orders [oai_citation:51‡docs.frappe.io](https://docs.frappe.io/erpnext/user/manual/en/e_commerce_settings#:~:text=Shopping%20Cart). So, if we want to allow the user to choose a branch and then show that branch’s pricing/stock on the website, we need to implement a solution.

**Gap:** Multi-store/branch selection is not available out of the box. This is a **significant gap** for matching OPD’s experience if they require a single website serving multiple dealers. Potential approaches:
- **Multi-Tenant Approach:** Deploy separate sites (one per branch) on Frappe Cloud, each with its own ERPNext company and Webshop, but share a common product catalog via periodic sync. This could be heavy and defeat the “single unified system” benefit.
- **Single Site, Multi-Company:** Use one ERPNext site with multiple companies (one per branch). We could then customize the Webshop front-end to allow a “Select Store” (perhaps store as a session or cookie). Based on the selection, we’d switch the context:
  - Choose the appropriate Price List for that branch (each branch/company can maintain its own price list for items).
  - Filter stock availability to that branch’s warehouse or simply show generic availability but assign orders to the nearest warehouse.
  - When the user checks out, set the Company (branch) on the Sales Order according to the selection.
